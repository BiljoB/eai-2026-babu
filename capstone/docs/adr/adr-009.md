# ADR-009 — Wire tap for hop records, with causation carried in headers

**Status:** accepted
**Date:** 2026-11-20

## Context

One order passes through four services, two queues' retry paths, up to four provider calls and possibly a DLQ replay. When an order ends `parked` or `compensated`, the operator needs one ordered story: which hop ran, which caused which, how long each took, and where it failed. The logs of four containers do not give that. They have no shared key, their clocks and formats differ, and nothing says that a `saga.retry` was caused by a particular consume attempt. The trace must also survive a restart of any service, and it must never make the business flow slower or less reliable.

## Decision

**Hop records and the wire tap.** Every service builds a hop `{hopId, correlationId, causationId, service, operation, status, attempt, messageId, startedAt, finishedAt, durationMs, detail}` (`<shared/trace.ts>`) and publishes it to the fanout exchange `trace.events`. The queue `trace.store` is bound to it, and the observer consumes that queue and appends each hop to `/data/trace-store.jsonl`. `GET /trace/:cid` filters that file by `correlationId` and sorts by `startedAt`. The hop's `hopId` is generated before the work starts, so it can be used as a causation id.

**Publishing never blocks business work.** The hop is published fire-and-forget: `<trace.publish()>` is called without awaiting a confirm, wrapped in try/catch, and any error is only logged. A failure of the broker channel, a missing exchange or a serialization error cannot fail an order or a nack.

**Causation across the boundaries.**
- *Message:* the publishing hop's `hopId` goes into `headers.causationId` (`intake.publish`, `saga.outcome`, `saga.dead_letter`, `notify.dead_letter`, `dlq.replay`). The consumer's first hop (`saga.consume`, `notify.consume`) takes that header as its `causationId`.
- *Retry:* `nack(msg, false, false)` cannot rewrite headers, so every delivery attempt carries the same `causationId`, the publisher's hop. Attempts differ by `attempt`, read from `x-death`. Each failed attempt writes a `saga.retry` or `notify.retry` hop caused by that attempt's consume hop.
- *Replay:* `dlq.replay` is caused by the DLQ message's `causationId` (its `*.dead_letter` hop). The replayed message carries the `dlq.replay` hop's id.
- *Mock call:* the step hop's `hopId` is generated before the HTTP call and sent as `X-Causation-Id`, so the mock's `/admin/logs` can be matched to the hop.
- *Compensation:* `inventory_release` is caused by the failing step's hop, and `payment_refund` by `inventory_release` when both run.

## Alternatives considered

- **OpenTelemetry.** It is the standard, but auto-instrumentation does not understand a message that is nacked into a TTL queue and redelivered, and it needs a collector container. The brief also forbids it as a replacement for our own header propagation. Rejected.
- **Scatter-gather.** The observer would ask every service for its hops when someone requests a trace. It needs every service to be up and to keep its own records, and it loses the trace of a service that is down. The wire tap stores hops centrally and continuously.
- **Confirmed hop publishing.** It would guarantee the broker accepted each hop, but a slow or blocked broker channel would add latency to every step, and a trace failure could fail an order. Rejected: tracing is observation and must not be able to hurt the thing it observes.
- **Writing hops directly to the observer by HTTP.** It couples every service to the observer's uptime, and it loses hops while the observer is down.

## Consequences

**What a lost hop costs.** Fire-and-forget means a hop can be lost if the channel is closed or the broker is unreachable at that moment. The order still completes, but its trace has a gap, and a causation link may then point to a hop that is not stored. The operator sees a hole, not a wrong story. I saw this when `<describe: e.g. I stopped the broker and a trace was missing hops>`.

**When the observer is down.** Hops keep accumulating in `trace.store`, which is durable and has no consumer. When the observer returns it drains the queue and appends them, so nothing is lost, only delayed. While it is down, `/trace` and `/ops/metrics` are unavailable and `trace.store` grows. If the observer is down for long, that queue's growth must be watched. The observer's own restart does not lose hops it already wrote, because the file is on a named volume.

**When the broker is down.** Hops are dropped, and services answer 503 on `/health`. After the reconnect, new hops flow again, so the trace of an order that straddled the outage may be incomplete.

**Other costs.** Every unit of work adds one extra publish, and the trace file is append-only and grows without limit. A retry's hops share the publisher's `causationId`, so the attempts are told apart only by `attempt`, not by the causation chain. A duplicated hop from an at-least-once redelivery is possible, since the observer does not dedupe by `hopId`.
